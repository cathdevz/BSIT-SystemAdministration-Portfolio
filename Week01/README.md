## Challenges Encountered

1. **Downloading large ISO files.** The Ubuntu and Windows evaluation ISO files are very large, and I had to make sure my internet connection was stable and that I had enough space on my drive before downloading them. I solved this by checking my available storage first and downloading one file at a time so the download would not fail halfway. This reminded me that as a System Administrator, managing storage and planning ahead is part of the job.

2. **My laptop was very slow.** While installing the software and taking screenshots, my laptop kept lagging and every application took a long time to open. It was hard to work because I had to wait every few minutes. I solved this by closing the programs I was not using, freeing up some space on my drive, and being patient with each installation. I also learned that this is exactly what a System Administrator deals with, making sure a computer has enough resources to run the tools needed for the job.

## Reflection

This activity taught me the importance of building a professional presence online. Setting up my GitHub and LinkedIn accounts made me realize how much of my future career depends on how I present myself digitally. As an aspiring IT professional, it is not enough to just know how to use tools. I also need to show my skills to the world. LinkedIn allows me to connect with other professionals, share what I am learning, and grow my network, which will be very useful when I start looking for a job or an internship. GitHub, on the other hand, serves as my portfolio where all my projects and activities are stored and visible to anyone who wants to see my work.

I also learned that these platforms are not just for showing off. They are also for learning. By following other people and seeing how they document their work, I can improve my own skills. Having a complete GitHub profile and an active LinkedIn account makes me look more professional and ready for the industry, and I plan to keep them updated as I progress through this course.

I also appreciated learning how to document properly using Markdown. Writing my own README made me realize that technical documentation is a skill on its own. If my files are not organized and my documentation is not clear, even the best work can look messy. As a future System Administrator, I know I will be responsible for documentation, backups, and configuration, so learning these habits now is a big advantage.

I believe this portfolio will help me grow throughout the semester. Every activity I do from now on will be added here, and by the end of the semester, I will have a collection of work that proves what I have learned. I am looking forward to the next weeks and to learning more about virtualization, Linux, and system maintenance. This first week gave me the foundation I needed, and I am excited to keep building on top of it.
